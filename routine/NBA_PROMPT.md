## 4. Modeling (write real code, in python)
### 4.0 Objective
The objective is HIT RATE: maximize the joint probability that each parlay wins, with EV as a guardrail plus the profit floor below. Core parlays are 2-3 legs on purpose: never add legs.
- Very short legs pay too little: a 2-leg parlay of -800 legs pays about +25 and needs an 80%+ hit rate. The sweet spot for legs is about -250 to -650. Use a leg shorter than -700 only when it is clearly the best way to keep the parlay's EV up, and never more than one per parlay.
- Profit floor: every parlay must clear P(hit) x payout decimal >= 0.97 by your fair probabilities (expected loss no worse than 3%), aiming for >= 1.00. Report that number for every parlay. If a parlay can't meet both the floor and the joint target, publish fewer parlays (down to the minimum in 1) and say so.
- Same-game pairs cost money: the app trims 20% of a same-game group's profit. Put every leg in a different game unless your sims show the positive correlation raises the joint probability by more than the trim costs (rare at these prices).
- Moneylines: favorites' moneylines are main markets with the least vig, and Pinnacle prices them sharply. A favorite at about -200 to -550 whose Pinnacle no-vig win probability is >= 0.72, with a DK/FD price no worse than 2 points off that, is a prime leg, but only when its stars are confirmed or very likely to play.
- Staking: size each parlay's `stake` by confidence, using only 50, 100, 150 or 200. The slate's total stake must not exceed $100 x the number of parlays, so you move money from weaker parlays to stronger ones, not bet more. Rank parlays by EV at the app's payout (P(hit) x payout decimal - 1), breaking ties by P(hit):
  - $200 or $150: only parlays with EV >= +2% AND P(hit) at or above the "aim for" target, and only when every player in them is listed as available (not questionable or probable with a real injury) and Pinnacle agrees with your side. Give $200 only to the single best parlay of the day, and only when it clearly beats the rest.
  - $100: parlays that meet the targets with EV roughly between -1% and +2%.
  - $50: parlays that only just clear the profit floor (EV below -1%) or carry extra uncertainty (a questionable teammate whose absence would change the role, a back-to-back rest risk, a new role).
  - If nothing qualifies for more than $100, stake every parlay at $100. Never raise a stake to chase a loss.
  In nba/lessons.md, track profit by stake tier each week. If after 3 or more weeks the $150-$200 parlays aren't winning at least as often as their stated P(hit), shrink the spread back toward flat $100 and say why.
- News risk (the morning slate is built before final injury news; the late-swap routine can replace dead legs before tip, but can't fix a leg that loses to a role change): only use player props on players listed as available or not on the injury report, with no minutes restriction, in a stable role for their last 5 games. No props on any player whose team has a questionable or doubtful starter whose absence would clearly change that player's minutes or usage, unless your projection already covers both outcomes and the leg clears in both. No props on a team's players on the second night of a back-to-back if that team has rested players in that spot this season. Team legs need the same care: a favorite's spread or moneyline depends on its stars playing.
- Leg cost: read `history/nba_backtest.md` when it exists. It measures, from 2023-2026 historical NBA odds, what each market and price range cost at DraftKings (hit rate vs implied), whether DK prices cheaper than the other books' median win, whether morning prices beat the close, and how well favorites' win probabilities hold up. Apply its numbers here the way the NFL prompt applies its backtest (routine/PROMPT.md, Leg cost): quote each market's measured cost in the report, prefer the cheapest markets, and record which you used. Until that file exists, follow the NFL discipline, since NFL props cost about 7-10% per leg against about 1-3% for team markets:
  - Team legs first: at least 60% of each slate's legs are team markets (moneylines, spreads/alt spreads, totals/alt totals, team totals/alt team totals, halves and first quarters).
  - Props need a reason. Each player-prop leg needs a specific, written edge beyond "projection clears the rung" (a teammate's absence the market hasn't fully priced, a minutes or usage change, a matchup signal, Pinnacle or the consensus disagreeing with DK in your favor). At most 1 prop per parlay, except where needed to meet the 4b variety minimum, or when a second prop is priced 3+ implied points cheaper at DK than the `cs` consensus.
  - Price shopping: compare DK's implied probability to the median implied probability of the other books in `cs` (exclude BetOnline). Prefer legs where DK is at least 1-2 points cheaper than that median. Skip any leg where DK is 1+ points MORE expensive than the median unless nothing comparable exists. Record each leg's gap as `cg` (consensus gap, in implied points, + = DK cheaper) on the leg in nba/picks.json and in nba/history/legs.csv.
  - Expected cost: for every parlay, sum the measured cost of its legs and state it in the report. A parlay only makes sense if your edge on its legs plausibly covers that cost.
- Leg pool: legs whose fair probability is >= 0.65 (prefer 0.72-0.88). Typical sources: main-line sides where your model and the market agree, alternate spreads and totals bought down to a safer number, team totals the model clearly clears, favorites' moneylines, alt-ladder player rungs well below the player's median (e.g. 15+ points for a starter projected for 22 in 34 minutes, 6+ rebounds for a center projected for 10). Plus-money legs only if fair >= 0.65 anyway (rare).
- Avoid high-variance outcomes: threes Overs on players under about 6 attempts a game, steals, blocks, blocks+steals, turnovers, first-quarter and first-half player props, double-doubles, high alt rungs, Unders on stars in competitive games, and props on bench players whose minutes swing with game script. Use one only if fair >= 0.70.
- Blowout risk: for any player prop, model the chance the game is decided by 15+ (from the spread distribution) and the minutes the player loses in that case. Starters on big favorites lose the most. Overs on starters in games with a spread of 12+ need fair >= 0.72.
- Sharp baseline: every odds response carries `px`, Pinnacle's prices for the same markets (reference only, not bettable). When Pinnacle has the market, its de-vigged price IS your market baseline; otherwise (or if `px` is missing) use the `cs` consensus in 4.1. A leg where Pinnacle's fair probability is clearly above DK/FD's implied probability is a strong pick; a leg where Pinnacle disagrees with you is a red flag.
- Calibration: your fair probability is the de-vigged market probability shaded by your model, never moved more than 6 percentage points from it (alt rungs: de-vig the ladder by fitting a distribution to the main line and ladder, then shade). Trust the market.
- EV guardrail: each leg's fair probability must be at least the vig-free implied probability of its price minus 2 points.
- Joint-probability targets (after correlation): 2-leg >= 0.58 (aim for 0.62+), 3-leg >= 0.48 (aim for 0.52+), plus the profit floor. If the board can't reach a target, publish the highest-probability parlay you can build and say so in the note. Within a game, prefer POSITIVELY correlated legs (a high total + a team total Over; a fast-paced game + a point guard's assists rung; a favorite's spread + its star's points rung only if blowout risk doesn't cut his minutes); never pair negatively correlated legs (a big favorite's alt spread with its starters' Overs is often negative because of bench time).
- Injury/role risk is the #1 killer of high-probability NBA legs: re-check every chosen player's status in the last 10 minutes before pushing.

### 4.1 Models
- Market baseline: de-vig every two-way market (both sides). Use Pinnacle when it has the market. Otherwise build a consensus from `cs`: de-vig each book that posts both sides and take the median no-vig probability; for Over-only alt ladders, fit the ladder at each book (anchored to that book's two-sided main line) and take the median. Compare DK's price to that baseline for every candidate leg: DK at or better than the consensus is a plus, and DK clearly worse than the consensus is a reason to pass. Report how many books priced each chosen leg.
- Game-line history: from the ESPN `pickcenter` closing spreads and totals you collected (this season and last; plus history/nba_backtest.md when it exists), price alternate spreads, alternate totals, team totals and first-half/first-quarter lines from the empirical distribution of results around similar closing lines, smoothed, not only a normal curve (NBA margins have no strong key numbers but do have fat tails in blowouts). Check it against nba/history/legs.csv as that grows.
- Team model: opponent-adjusted offensive and defensive ratings per 100 possessions and pace from your box-score logs (season and last 10 games, weighted toward recent), home court about 2 points, rest and travel adjustments (0c), and availability adjustments: for each missing or limited player, the value lost given who replaces his minutes (on/off and box-score impact estimates; check against how far the market moved when he was ruled out before). Projected margin and total -> cover/over probabilities with error drawn from the empirical residuals (margin sd about 12-13, total sd about 18-19 as a starting point). Blend with the market, weighting the market heavily.
- Player props: project minutes first (role, recent minutes, rest spot, blowout distribution from the game sim, foul tendencies, teammates out), then per-minute rates (points, rebounds, assists, threes, combos) weighted toward recent games, adjusted for opponent and pace and for usage redistribution when teammates are out. Model counts with suitable distributions (e.g. negative binomial or empirical per-minute sampling), and price every alt rung with P(over).
- Simulate every game jointly (Monte Carlo, at least 10,000 sims per game) so team and player outcomes share one game script (pace, margin, minutes); use the sims for same-game correlations and blowout effects.
- Parlay payout = product of decimal odds, with each same-game group's profit multiplied by 0.8 (app rule). Report P(hit), payout and EV for each parlay.
- Adversarial review before publishing: for each chosen leg, write the strongest case for how it loses (late scratch, minutes restriction, blowout benching, foul trouble, a cold shooting night for a threes-dependent leg, stale data) and replace it if that case is realistic.
- Build each parlay to MAXIMIZE JOINT HIT PROBABILITY at the required leg count while clearing the profit floor, spreading risk across games, never reusing an identical parlay from earlier slates this week.

## 5. Rules every parlay must satisfy (the app enforces them)
- Only games that haven't tipped off (compare `t` to now) and are still on the board.
- Copy each leg exactly from a response you fetched this run: `ev`, `home`, `away`, `t`, `mk`, `n`, plus `d` and `p` when present, and `o`. Never invent or round lines or prices.
- Don't stack near-duplicate outcomes in one parlay: a team's moneyline with its own spread, a team total with the game total in the same direction, or a player's points with a combo that includes his points (points+rebounds, points+assists, PRA), and the same for rebounds and assists. The app's flat same-game trim doesn't price that overlap.
- No two legs in one parlay may share game + market family + player/team (both sides, Over and Under, a main and alt line on the same stat). Market family = key with `alternate_` / `_alternate` removed.
- Every market must be gradable from an ESPN NBA box score (points, rebounds, assists, threes made, steals, blocks, turnovers and their combos; team and game scores by half/quarter). Nothing else (no first basket, no double-doubles).

## 5b. Pick'ems (straight-up winners, no spread)
The app's "Claude's Pick'ems" page needs your outright winner for EVERY NBA game tipping today (ET date) that hasn't tipped yet, ignoring the line, including games you left out of the parlays. This uses data you already fetched (no extra credits).
- Apply what nba/lessons.md says about past pick'em misses.
- Near coin flips: when the de-vigged moneyline gives the favorite 0.50-0.58, pick the market favorite. Pick the other side only for specific fresh news the line hasn't absorbed yet (a star ruled out or returning, a rest decision), and name that news in the `why`. Your power model alone isn't enough to flip one. Track near-coin-flip picks as their own row in the calibration table.
- For each game, pick the team you think wins outright. Start from the de-vigged moneyline win probability, then adjust with your team model (ratings, home court, rest, availability). Pick the underdog only when your blended model gives it > 50%, and say why.
- `w` = the winner's team name copied exactly from the game's `home` or `away`. `conf` = your win probability for that team (0.50-0.99, 2 decimals). `why` = one short sentence with the key reason and a number (e.g. "Model 66% vs 61% de-vigged ML; Denver rested, Murray back from a 3-game absence.").
