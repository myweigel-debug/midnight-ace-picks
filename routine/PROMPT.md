### 4.0 Objective (read first; this replaces the old EV-maximizing approach)
Earlier slates maximized EV and produced plus-money long shots (joint hit chances of 3-40%, ~16% average, legs mostly +100 to +220). They didn't win a single parlay and embarrassed the owner in front of friends. The objective is now HIT RATE: the owner expects most parlays to win every week, so maximize the joint probability that each parlay wins. EV is a guardrail, plus the profit floor below. Core parlays are 2-3 legs on purpose: never add legs. The only exception is the Sunday bonus parlays below.

ROI lessons from Week 9 (the first hit-rate week: parlays 11-1, +$368 on $1,200, while the slates' own fair probabilities expected a small loss, so it ran hot; about two-thirds of the legs with a closing price closed shorter than we bet them, a good sign the legs were well chosen):
- Very short legs pay too little. Sunday's legs were mostly -740 to -900, so each 2-leg paid only about +25: break-even needs an 80% hit rate, yet those parlays were only about 76% to hit, so they lost money on average and one miss wiped out four wins. The Tue/Thu legs at -420 to -600 paid +42 to +80 at nearly break-even or better EV. The sweet spot for legs is about -250 to -650. Use a leg shorter than -700 only when it is clearly the best way to keep the parlay's EV up, and never more than one per parlay.
- Every parlay must clear a profit floor as well as the hit-rate target: P(hit) x payout decimal >= 0.97 by your fair probabilities (expected loss no worse than 3%), aiming for >= 1.00. Report that number for every parlay. If a parlay can't meet both the floor and the joint target, publish fewer parlays rather than force one (minimum 2 on Tue/Thu, 4 on Sunday), and say so in the note. A $100 bet you expect to lose money on is a loss for the owner's board.
- Same-game pairs cost money: the app trims 20% of a same-game group's profit, and this week's two same-game parlays had the worst EV of the slate (-5% and -8%). Put every leg in a different game unless your sims show the positive correlation raises the joint probability by more than the trim costs (rare at these prices).
- Moneylines are welcome where they help the odds of winning. Favorites' moneylines are main markets with the least vig, and Pinnacle prices them sharply. A favorite at about -200 to -550 whose Pinnacle no-vig win probability is >= 0.72, with a DK/FD price no worse than 2 points off that, is a prime leg, often better than an alt prop at -800. Pick'ems went 13-1 against roughly 9.5 expected from their stated confidence, so treat that streak as mostly luck: it doesn't make moneylines safer than their prices say, and every moneyline still has to beat the other legs on the same bar.
- Fragile low-volume markets lose even at short prices: Kamari Lassiter (CB) 2+ solo tackles at -900 was the week's only miss (1 solo). For defense legs prefer high-snap linebackers and box safeties on tackles+assists rungs, not cornerbacks' solo tackles.

  - Price shopping: compare DK's implied probability to the median implied probability of the other books in `cs` (exclude BetOnline). Prefer legs where DK is at least 1-2 points cheaper than that median; team legs 2+ points cheaper lost only about 1% in the backtest. Skip any leg where DK is 1+ points MORE expensive than the median unless nothing comparable exists. Record each leg's gap as `cg` (consensus gap, in implied points, + = DK cheaper) on the leg in picks.json (an extra field; the app ignores it) and in history/legs.csv (add the column to the header if it's missing).
  - Expected cost: for every parlay, sum the backtest cost of its legs (for example 3 team legs at about 3% plus 1 prop at about 8%) and state it in the notes. A parlay only makes sense if your edge on its legs plausibly covers that cost.
  - The pick'em backtest confirms the market favorite wins at about its stated rate (50-60% favorites won 55-61%), which supports the 5b near-coin-flip rule.
- Leg pool: legs whose fair probability is >= 0.65 (prefer 0.72-0.88). Typical sources: main-line sides where your model and the market agree, alternate spreads/totals bought down to a safer number, alt-ladder player rungs well below the player's median (e.g. 40+ yds for a receiver projected for 65), favorites' moneylines, team totals the model clearly clears. Plus-money legs only if fair >= 0.65 anyway (rare).
- Avoid high-variance outcomes: anytime/first TD scorers, interceptions, longest-reception/rush, high alt rungs, unders on boom-or-bust players. Use one only if fair >= 0.70.
- Sharp baseline: every odds response also carries `px`, Pinnacle's prices for the same markets (reference only, not bettable in the app). Pinnacle is the sharpest book: when it has the market, its de-vigged price IS your market baseline; otherwise (or if `px` is missing) de-vig DK/FD. A leg where Pinnacle's fair probability is clearly above DK/FD's implied probability is a strong pick; a leg where Pinnacle disagrees with you is a red flag.
- Calibration: your fair probability is the de-vigged market probability shaded by your model, never moved more than 6 percentage points from it (alt rungs: de-vig the ladder by fitting a distribution to the main line and ladder, then shade). Past slates claimed 4-8 point edges that didn't exist; trust the market.
- EV guardrail: each leg's fair probability must be at least the vig-free implied probability of its price minus 2 points. Don't chase EV beyond that.
- Joint-probability targets (after correlation): 2-leg >= 0.58 (aim for 0.62+), 3-leg >= 0.48 (aim for 0.52+), and the profit floor above. If the board can't reach a target, publish the highest-probability parlay you can build and say so in the note. Within a game, prefer POSITIVELY correlated legs (favorite's alt spread + its RB's rush-yard rung; a high total + the QB's pass-yard rung; a heavy-run opponent + a LB's tackles rung) since they raise the joint hit chance; never pair negatively correlated legs.
- Injury/role risk is the #1 killer of high-probability legs: only use players with no injury designation (or a full-practice Friday), a stable role/snap share over the last 3 weeks, and no weather risk for their stat. Re-check every chosen player in the last 10 minutes before pushing.

### 4.1 Models
- Market baseline: de-vig every two-way market (both sides). Use Pinnacle when it has the market. Otherwise build a consensus from `cs`: de-vig each book that posts both sides and take the median no-vig probability; for Over-only alt ladders, fit the ladder at each book and take the median. Compare DK's price to that baseline for every candidate leg: DK at or better than consensus is a plus, and DK clearly worse than consensus (the book is shading that side) is a reason to pass. Report how many books priced each chosen leg.
- Game-line history: games.csv also has every game's closing spread, total and final score since 1999. Price alternate spreads, alternate totals, team totals and half lines from the empirical distribution of results around similar closing lines (2015+, smoothed; margins cluster on 3, 7, 10, 6 and 14), not only a normal curve: e.g. P(favorite wins by 4+ | closing spread -6.5) from the games with spreads near -6.5. Check it against `history/legs.csv` as that grows.
- Team model: opponent-adjusted power ratings from season data (point differential and per-play efficiency from your box-score logs; EPA if nflverse is reachable), home field about 1.5 pts, rest and travel, QB and key-player injury adjustments, weather (see 0c). Projected margin and total -> cover/over probabilities with normal error (margin sd ~13.5, total sd ~10). Blend with the market, weighting the market heavily.
- Player props: per-player distributions from game logs and role (targets, carries, snaps, tackles), weighted toward recent games, adjusted for opponent, game script, weather and teammate injuries. Price every alt rung with P(over); the safe low rungs are where most legs come from. Tackles: negative binomial from `defensive.totalTackles`/`soloTackles`, adjusted for opponent plays and run rate. Kickers: FG attempts/PATs/kicking points from a drive-level sim plus kicker accuracy and weather.
- Simulate every game jointly (Monte Carlo, at least 10,000 sims per game) so team and player outcomes share one game script; use the sims for same-game correlations.
- Parlay payout = product of decimal odds, with each same-game group's profit multiplied by 0.8 (app rule). Report P(hit), payout and EV for each parlay.
- Adversarial review before publishing: for each chosen leg, write the strongest case for how it loses (injury, role change, blowout pulling starters, weather, stale data) and replace it if that case is realistic.
- Build each parlay to MAXIMIZE JOINT HIT PROBABILITY at the required leg count while clearing the profit floor (prefer the -250 to -650 legs that pay enough to make winning worth it), spreading risk across games, never reusing an identical parlay from earlier slates this week.

## 4b. Position variety (owner's standing request)
The owner wants every slate to feature a variety of positions, like the parlays the owner's friends hit, but every leg must still meet the 4.0 bar (fair >= 0.65). Across each slate include at least one leg from each of: team bets (spread/alt spread/total/team total/moneyline/halves), QB, RB, and WR or TE. Sunday slates: at least 2 legs from each of those four groups when you publish the full 6 parlays; with fewer parlays, at least 1 from each group. Defensive-player and kicker legs are OPTIONAL: pick only the best ones, never to fill variety. Use one only when it ranks among the strongest legs on the whole board AND it is priced by at least 3 books in `cs` (or Pinnacle) with DK at or better than the consensus no-vig price AND it comes from a high-volume role (defense: an every-down linebacker or box safety on a tackles+assists rung, sacks only if fair >= 0.70; kicker: a reliable kicker in good weather with an offense that reaches field-goal range, on a low FG-made, PAT or kicking-points rung). It's fine for a slate to have none. Variety never overrides the profit floor. Mix positions within parlays too: no parlay with 3+ legs should be all one position group (the Sunday 5-leg moneyline parlay is the one exception); defense and kicker each count as their own group for that check. The bonus parlays' legs don't count toward the variety tally. Validate this in python. For each group, take its highest-probability legs; if a group truly has nothing at >= 0.65, use its best leg at >= 0.60 and say so in the report. Never use a long-shot leg to fill variety.

## 5. Rules every parlay must satisfy (the app enforces them)
- Only games that haven't kicked off (compare `t` to now) and are still on the board.
- Copy each leg exactly from a response you fetched this run: `ev`, `home`, `away`, `t`, `mk`, `n`, plus `d` and `p` when present, and `o`. Never invent or round lines or prices.
- Don't stack near-duplicate outcomes in one parlay, such as a team's moneyline with its own spread, or a QB's pass yards with the same QB's pass+rush yards. The app's flat same-game trim doesn't price that overlap, so it would inflate the payout unfairly; win on picks, not on app quirks.
- No two legs in one parlay may share game + market family + player/team (both sides, Over and Under, a main and alt line on the same stat, two first-TD scorers). Market family = key with `alternate_` / `_alternate` removed.
- Avoid player_pass_longest_completion (can't be auto-graded).
